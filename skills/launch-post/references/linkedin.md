# LinkedIn post

## Limits and facts

- The feed shows roughly the first 2–3 lines (about 140–210 characters) before "see more". The first line is the hook:
  ≤ 120 characters, and it must make sense alone.
- Upload the video natively (`li-4x5` or `li-1x1`). It autoplays muted; burned captions carry the story. You can also
  attach the SRT as captions for accessibility.
- 3–6 short lines (paragraph breaks between them). 60–150 words total. Hashtags 0–2 at the end, default 0.
- Links in the body are fine; some founders put the link in the first comment. Follow the user's habit.

## Structure

1. Hook line (≤ 120 chars): the problem or the outcome in the reader's words.
2. What it does (1–2 lines): concrete verb + object + who it is for.
3. One specific (1 line): a sourced number, a before/after, or an honest limitation.
4. Why now / why you (optional, 1 line): first person, plain.
5. CTA + link (1 line): what to do, no pressure words.

## Acme example (fictional; write your own for the real product)

```
Freelancers lose a day a month chasing invoices.

Acme Ledger turns your timesheet into an invoice and sends the reminders on day 1, 7 and 14.
When the client pays, the invoice closes itself.

v1 is invoicing and reminders only. No bank sync yet.

I built it after a year of writing the same "just following up" email.
Free for your first 10 clients: acme-ledger.example
```
(First line 48 chars. "a day a month" must be a sourced claim in BRIEF.md, else rewrite the hook as a statement
of the user's own experience.)

## Rules

- No "I'm excited to announce", "Big news", "After months of hard work", "Thrilled to share" openers.
- No one-word-per-line staircase formatting. No bold Unicode text. No emoji bullets.
- No tagging of people or companies without their consent.
- Credit line (only if the user opted in): last line,
  `Video made with launchfilm, an open-source kit by Eugene Li: github.com/evgenspm/launchfilm`.
